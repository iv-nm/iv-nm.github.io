VideoScape: Bringing Videos to Your macOS Desktop

I always wanted to set a video as mac wallpaper. Whenever I attempted to use a video as a wallpaper, the apps I tried either restricted my choices or bogged me down with needless complexity. I wanted something simpler. A simple tool that alows to set live wallpapers for free

Building the Core

Designing a tool that integrates video playback with macOS’s wallpaper system wasn’t straightforward. Static images behave one way; videos behave in a totally different way—especially when it comes to the menu bar and multiple monitors. So I had to get creative with how I layered the video behind the icons, ensuring it felt seamless and avoided unnecessary resource usage, because mac is not provided with these features.

I explored several approaches: from creating hidden windows for each display to leveraging AVPlayer. Eventually, I found a system that balanced performance with flexibility—running a lightweight player on each screen, looping the video as needed, and caching a static frame so the desktop still looks consistent whenever the video fades in or out.

Setting videos

A major part of the experience comes from how VideoScape handles transitions and screen changes. When you drag a video into VideoScape, the app creates a security-scoped bookmark to ensure your chosen file remains accessible—no matter where you move it or how often you reboot your Mac. Then, it fetches a frame from the video to serve as a static preview. That way, your wallpaper never just “disappears” if you pause or toggle off the live video.

I also focused on making multi-monitor setups smoother, so VideoScape replicates the wallpaper across every screen with minimal overhead.

Overcoming the Menu Bar Challenge

MacOS menu bar wasn't same color as the video. This happens because macOS isn’t natively optimized for video wallpapers; it’s expecting static images. I experimented with color sampling and system toggles, but the truth is, until Apple opens the door for live desktop adjustments, it remains a bit finicky.

While it’s not perfect, but it's the smoothest solution I could made for now. Before set the video, I make static stopframe of it and set it as static wallpaper, then I use video as overlay. Analyzing how other apps work, I understtod that they do essentially the same thing

Designing the VideoScape Icon

I went through multiple iterations—concept sketches. Eventually, I settled on an icon that feels simple and clean, hinting at the app’s core purpose: bringing videos easily to your desktop.
